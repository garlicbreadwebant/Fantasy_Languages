# Lingua Ignota: design and source notes

## Existing language; new learning collection

This project documents Lingua Ignota; it does not claim to have designed or standardized it. Fantasy Languages is the collection title; these languages have different historical and community purposes.

## Evidence

[Diplomatic manuscript transcription](http://philipneal.net/voynichsources/lingua_ignota_hildegard/), based on Steinmeyer and Sievers. [Manuscript catalogue](https://geschichtsquellen.de/werk/2812). English glosses are editorial translations of individual Latin glosses.

## Editorial policy

Aigonz is not a greeting. The overview displays it as a documented first word. A missing equivalent means this edition has no verified mapping; it does not prove the word never existed.

Every collection uses the exact 397 English lookup labels frozen from Universal_Languages on October 18, 2026. Shared English labels do not guarantee exact semantic equivalence. Notes distinguish contextual descriptions, historical vocabulary, grammatical constructions, and unresolved equivalents. A dash is an explicit editorial gap or a lack of direct correspondence; it must never be pronounced as a target word.

No unverified form should be described as attested. No language should inherit another language’s grammar. AI assistance must respect these limits and decline to invent missing vocabulary.

## Current coverage

26 supplied learning forms or constructions; 371 unresolved or non-equivalent lookup entries. Supplied entries are a starter collection requiring speaker or historical specialist review, not a claim of authoritative translation.
